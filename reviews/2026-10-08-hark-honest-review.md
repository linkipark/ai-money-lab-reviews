# Hark Honest Review — I Let an AI Assistant Run My Web Chores for a Day

- **Published:** 2026-10-08
- **Video:** https://youtu.be/b3Mo3Oj1kgE (AI Money Lab)
- **Product:** Hark (https://hark.com) — "the operating system for your life"
- **Reviewer:** AI Money Lab

## Key facts

- Hark is an AI personal assistant with its own cloud computer: it does real-world web tasks (orders food, juggles email, schedules recurring tasks, registers accounts) while you watch its live browser view.
- Founded by serial entrepreneur Brett Adcock (Figure AI), who self-funded Hark with $100 million of his own money.
- Head of design is Abidur Chowdhury — the Apple designer who led the iPhone Air and narrated its reveal, then left Apple to join Hark.
- Hark Pro launched October 6, 2026 (web, iPhone, Android) — two days before this review.
- Pricing: $0 / $20 / $100 per month — but the tiers are described only as 2× and 10× more usage. Hark publishes zero exact message or credit numbers.
- Hark's own marketing copy: $20/month buys "40 organic-produce deliveries and 40 pizza orders"; $100/month buys "50 weddings, 100 grand pianos, 35 cross-country moves, and 250 lawyers."
- All features are offered in all plans. Upgrading only increases usage — the free tier is never feature-gated, and never shows ads.
- The agent can run up to 36 browsers at once (e.g. comparing dentists or hotels in parallel).
- Privacy pledge: your data is yours, never sold, never goes to advertisers, deletable anytime.
- No referral or invite program exists as of 2026-10-08 (every settings, wallet, and billing section checked — no Invite/Referral/Share entry anywhere).
- Real test: Hark registered real accounts end-to-end (including a Muse account), working through signup flows and reading email verification codes from its own inbox via screenshots.
- Its cloud browser browsed Amazon without issues — a site that blocks most automation outright.
- One continuous chat thread holds everything; Projects give long-horizon tasks their own history and files; scheduled tasks run in the background.

## What's good

| # | Strength | Detail |
|---|----------|--------|
| 1 | A cloud browser you can watch | Live visible browser view with Cancel/Minimize controls — you see it scroll real sites |
| 2 | Completes multi-step web chores | Account signups, verification codes from its own email, real tasks end-to-end — not just answers |
| 3 | Persistent memory by design | Greets you by name, remembers preferences, people, and plans across sessions |
| 4 | Generous free tier | Full capabilities free, no ads, never feature-gated — upgrades only multiply usage |
| 5 | Single thread + long-horizon tools | One continuous chat; Projects with history/files; scheduled recurring tasks |

## The flaw

Opaque limits. No published message, credit, or task numbers, and no in-app usage meter — "you may reach a usage cap" is the entire disclosure. For a usage-metered product, that's a real transparency problem: you can't budget or plan around an invisible ceiling, you discover it only by hitting it.

## Smaller gripes

- Policy guardrails can refuse legitimate bulk work — a batch account-registration request was rejected as "farming." Sensible anti-abuse, but the line between power use and abuse is discovered only by refusal.
- Slow task spin-up: one test sat at "Specializing my approach" for ~4 minutes before the browser even opened. Minor UI bugs too (a stuck dialog with an obscured Close button).

## Verdict

Hark is the first AI assistant that genuinely does web chores for you through a visible cloud browser you can watch work — with a generous, never-feature-gated free tier. But opaque usage caps, slow spin-up, and unpredictable policy refusals mean power users will hit invisible walls.
